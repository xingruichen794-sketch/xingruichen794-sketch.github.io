The challenge

Humanoids need to move through a room while maintaining stable contact with objects. A controller that tracks joint motion alone is difficult to connect to diverse planners and manipulation skills.

CEER exposes an end-effector–root interface: high-level modules specify end-effector pose targets and root motion commands, while a shared low-level policy handles whole-body control.

My contribution

  • Simulation scene development. Built room-scale Isaac Sim scenes with randomized furniture layouts and box positions for testing humanoid manipulation behavior.
  • Box-manipulation evaluation. Tested humanoid box grasping, transport, and placement in simulation, supporting evaluation of the integrated loco-manipulation system.
  • Prototyping and testing. Contributed to end-effector design and 3D printing, and completed more than 10 hours of hardware testing and data collection.
  • Research collaboration. Co-authored the CEER paper, connecting the simulation work to the broader hierarchical control and evaluation effort.

The paper reports 3.3 cm end-effector tracking accuracy, reduced motion jerk relative to baselines, and up to 70% success on simulated single-object loco-manipulation tasks. Hardware experiments cover grasping, writing, wiping, cart pulling, and box transportation.

These figures describe the complete collaborative system. They are not individual performance claims, and the 70% result applies to the paper’s simulated single-object task setting.
