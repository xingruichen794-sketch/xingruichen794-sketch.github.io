The challenge

A planned stroke and a mark on paper are different things. Joint positioning error, coordinate mapping, contact conditions, and pastel wear all affect what the robot actually draws. This project connects image-based planning to physical execution and measures those sources of variation.

The initial workshop pipeline established open-loop painting. The subsequent technical report extends the work to joint-space calibration, checkpoint-based visual observation, bounded geometric correction, and fixed-angle wear modeling.

What I built

  • Image-to-motion pipeline. Extracted color regions and boundaries, generated boundary-tracing and contour-loop filling paths, and converted them into stroke commands and joint trajectories.
  • Workspace calibration. Collected a 7 × 7 grid at 20 mm spacing over a 120 × 120 mm workspace. Bilinear interpolation of joint angles maps positions inside each grid cell to robot commands.
  • Repeatable execution and observation. Integrated tool lowering, drawing, lifting, and parking for overhead image capture. Registered canvas images and kept original reference paths separate from corrected commands.
  • Control and material-state analysis. Tuned joint PID gains, evaluated angular positioning errors, and developed a fixed-angle geometric model relating pastel mass loss to contact area and paper-normal tool recession.

Across five joints, pooled mean absolute angular error decreased from 0.527° to 0.279°, a 47.1% reduction. The 90th-percentile error decreased from 1.618° to 0.527°. Each joint contributed 24 positioning measurements.

Current scope and next steps

The drawing workflow captures the canvas after batches of at most three strokes and can apply bounded corrections to unexecuted trajectories when observation checks pass. The fixed-angle wear model uses a 43° tool inclination; predicting deposited stroke width still requires separate empirical calibration.

Next steps are broader workspace validation, independent wear-model measurements, and integration of material-state estimates into drawing control. Aesthetic reasoning and automatic tool choice remain research directions.
