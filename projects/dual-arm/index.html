The coordination problem

Both arms can reach every cell of the 3 × 3 board. This flexibility creates a shared-workspace problem: independently planned motions can interfere even when each target is kinematically reachable. The project combines offline workspace analysis with board-region allocation and controlled access to the shared center column.

My contributions

I worked on perception-to-action integration, MATLAB kinematic modeling, and simulation and hardware validation within the team project.

  • Perception-to-action integration. Connected RGB-D board-state recognition with motion control in a closed-loop tic-tac-toe system.
  • Workspace analysis. Developed MATLAB models to check dual-arm reachability across the board.
  • Experimental integration. Evaluated the system in Gazebo and on the physical Kinova Gen3 Lite platform.

Perception, planning, and execution

  • Perception. The team pipeline transforms the overhead RGB-D point cloud into a common world frame using TF2, filters the board region and table plane, and clusters blocks with DBSCAN. Block centroids and region labels populate a YAML world-state file.
  • Task planning. The planner maps detected blocks to the nine cells, reconstructs the game state, and generates pick-and-place action scripts for the selected move and arm.
  • Execution. Separate ROS 2 namespaces isolate the left and right arms’ drivers, controllers, and MoveIt configurations.

Shared-workspace coordination

The board is divided into left and right territories, with the center column shared. A MutexNode admits only one arm to the shared region at a time. Staggered transport heights provide additional vertical separation during object transfer.

This team-level design combines a simple resource-access rule with geometric workspace allocation, reducing contention between the two motion-planning stacks.

Workspace analysis

The kinematic study queried inverse-kinematics solutions within joint limits for every cell and both arms. All nine targets were reachable by each manipulator, motivating explicit coordination of their overlapping workspaces.

Simulation and hardware validation

Validation progressed from MATLAB reachability analysis to Gazebo games and then physical trials. The perception, planning, and execution pipeline was reused on hardware with a calibrated camera-to-world transform and the physical board and robot placements.

The team report describes autonomous block placement and full-game execution without observed arm–arm contact in the reported trials. It does not give a trial count or a measured task-success rate.
