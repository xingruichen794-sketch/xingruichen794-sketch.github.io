The design problem

A humanoid waist joint needs substantial output torque within a constrained envelope. The thesis developed an RV reducer around a simplified humanoid load model, including a specified 10 kg hand payload and upper-body pitch and roll loading.

The work covers mechanical design and simulation. A manufactured reducer and measured transmission-accuracy results were outside the project scope.

Design work

  • Requirements and drivetrain sizing. Calculated waist-joint torque requirements, selected the drive motor, and allocated a total 75:1 reduction across a 2.5:1 gear stage and a 30:1 cycloidal stage.
  • Detailed mechanical design. Sized the input gear shaft, spur gears, crankshafts, cycloidal discs, pin housing, and bearings, and developed assembly and component drawings.
  • Joint integration. Reworked the pin-housing interface around an interference fit with the external joint housing to reduce the assembly envelope.
  • Analysis and manufacturing planning. Evaluated the simplified gear stage in ANSYS and analyzed machining, heat treatment, and finishing processes for critical parts.

The thesis reports a peak stress of approximately 82 MPa in its simplified gear-stage transient analysis, with a 1,500 rpm input and 3 N·m opposing load on each of three driven gears.

The dimensions, torque, mass, and accuracy above are design requirements. The simulated gear-stage result does not constitute validation of the full reducer under every operating condition. Backlash, efficiency, thermal behavior, and fatigue life were not verified on a physical prototype.

Engineering follow-up

The next engineering step is a manufactured prototype with dimensional inspection, transmission-error and backlash measurements, load testing, and thermal and fatigue evaluation. The thesis also identifies additional weight and size reduction opportunities after revisiting conservative component sizing.

Source: Design of RV Reducer for Waist Joint of Humanoid Robot, undergraduate thesis, Hefei University of Technology, May 2025; Sections 2, 3, and 6.
