The engineering task

Develop front and rear wings that improve aerodynamic efficiency while remaining practical to manufacture and integrate with a Formula Student race car.

My responsibilities

  • Team leadership. Led five teammates across two competition seasons in the aerodynamics and bodywork group.
  • Wing development. Designed front and rear wing geometries in CATIA and iterated multiple configurations using STAR-CCM+ aerodynamic simulations.
  • Composite fabrication. Contributed hands-on carbon-fiber layup and surface finishing. The first generation used machined PMI airfoil cores with carbon-fiber skins; the second generation used female-mold construction.

Outcome

2.9reported final downforce-to-drag ratio
2competition seasons
5teammates led

The reported downforce-to-drag ratio improved from 2.5 to 2.9 through aerodynamic iteration. Carbon-fiber aerodynamic components were manufactured as part of the race-car program.

The aerodynamic improvement is a simulation result; it is not presented as a measured reduction in lap time or a track-validated force measurement.

The associated first-author work, Analysis and Improvements of a Front Wing for Formula SAE Car, is listed in the résumé at CIAP 2024.
